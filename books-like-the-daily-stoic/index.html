Books like The Daily Stoic for founders, investors, and managers

The Daily Stoic made the one-page-a-day book popular again. If you decide for a living, five more in the same format are worth a year: The Daily Laws for power and people, The Daily Drucker for management, Limitless Daily for focus and learning, Tolstoy's A Calendar of Wisdom for the oldest version of the form, and Daily Wisdom for mental models, misjudgment, and money.

By Nasser Ghanemzadeh. Last updated October 2026.

The six books

1. The Daily Stoic, by Ryan Holiday and Stephen Hanselman (2016)

The book that brought the form back. Each day pairs a short passage from Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, Epictetus, or another Stoic with a page of commentary on how to use it now. The subject is the inner game: what you control, what you don't, and how to stay steady when the second category misbehaves. It is the right first daily book for almost anyone, which is also its limit. It says little about money, markets, or running a company.

2. The Daily Laws, by Robert Greene (2021)

Greene distilled his books on power, seduction, mastery, strategy, and human nature into a page a day, with each month built around one theme. Read it to understand people: why the colleague resists, why the negotiation stalls, why the board behaves as it does. It is sharper on reading others than on judging yourself.

3. The Daily Drucker, by Peter F. Drucker with Joseph A. Maciariello (2004)

366 readings drawn from Drucker's work on management, each ending with an action point. Time, decisions, innovation, the effective executive. The examples are from the twentieth century; the principles have aged better than most management writing since. The best daily book for anyone who runs a team.

4. A Calendar of Wisdom, by Leo Tolstoy

The oldest book here. Tolstoy spent his last years collecting thoughts from philosophers, prophets, and writers, grouped by a topic for each day, with his own reflections added. Peter Sekirin's English translation appeared in 1997. It is a book about how to live rather than how to decide, and it is the reminder that the daily form is a practice, not a modern productivity trick.

5. Limitless Daily, by Jim Kwik (2026)

From the author of Limitless, published in September 2026. Kwik turns his brain training into 366 short morning pages on the instrument you decide with, across five areas: mindset, focus, flow, learning, and habits. Each page is one small idea, built to be read in a few minutes before the day starts. It is the most practical book on this list about attention and learning, and the least about money, markets, or managing people.

6. Daily Wisdom, by Nasser Ghanemzadeh (2026)

366 one-page meditations for founders, investors, and managers, in the mental-model tradition of Charlie Munger and Peter Bevelin. Each page has three parts: a quotation checked against its primary source, an essay of about 250 words, and one question or action to carry into the day. The year is a course: the latticework of mental models in January, the psychology of misjudgment in February and March, then the mathematics of wisdom, money, the investor's temperament, the manager's craft, the founder's path, and a not-to-do list built from history's disasters. It is the one on this list written for decisions with money, runway, and other people's trust at stake. Full disclosure: I wrote this one.

How to choose

  • You want to stay steady under pressure. Start with The Daily Stoic.
  • You keep misreading people. The Daily Laws.
  • You run a team and want to run it better. The Daily Drucker.
  • Your bottleneck is focus or learning speed. Limitless Daily.
  • You want the long view, beyond work. A Calendar of Wisdom.
  • Your mistakes cost money: a hire, an investment, a partner, a burn rate. Daily Wisdom. Start with March 14, the pre-mortem, or June 8, the Cobra effect.

Two daily books at once is one too many. Pick the one that matches the decisions in front of you this year, and keep a notebook next to it. The prompts only pay if the writing happens.

Not daily, but the source

Two books sit underneath Daily Wisdom and much of this genre, and both reward a slow read after a year of pages. Peter Bevelin's Seeking Wisdom: From Darwin to Munger (2003) collects in one place what Darwin, Munger, Buffett, and a hundred scientists figured out about why we misjudge. Poor Charlie's Almanack gathers Charlie Munger's talks, including the one on the psychology of human misjudgment.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good alternative to The Daily Stoic for business?

For management, The Daily Drucker: 366 readings from Peter Drucker, each with an action point. For power and human nature, Robert Greene's The Daily Laws. For decisions about money, hiring, and risk, Daily Wisdom, which applies Charlie Munger's mental models and the psychology of misjudgment to founders, investors, and managers, one page a day.

Is there a daily book about mental models?

Yes. Daily Wisdom for Founders, Investors, and Managers is 366 one-page meditations in the mental-model tradition of Charlie Munger and Peter Bevelin. Each page has a quotation checked against its primary source, an essay of about 250 words, and one question or action for the day.

Do you have to start a daily book on January 1?

No. Each book on this list is organized by date, so you can start on today's page and go round the year from there. A second year through the same book reads differently from the first, because you are making different decisions.

AI use cases for B2B SaaS: 30 workflows, and which one to ship first

The AI use cases that actually ship in a B2B SaaS company are narrow: one user, one input, one output, and a person who checks the result. Below are 30 of them, grouped by team. Each is written as the one-sentence scope that opens a Sprint Brief, with the data it needs, its risk tier, and whether it fits a first ten-day Sprint. Most first workflows should be internal, and low or medium risk.

By Nasser Ghanemzadeh. Last updated October 2026.

How to read the list

Every entry follows the same sentence: for a user, when something happens, the workflow will do one thing and produce one output, so that something improves. If you can't fill those five slots for your idea, it isn't scoped yet.

  • Low risk

    Internal only. The people who review the output are the people who asked for it.

  • Medium risk

    Touches customer data, financial data, or appears in your product, with a person reviewing before anything leaves.

  • High risk

    Legal, compliance, payments, or autonomous actions toward customers. Not a first Sprint.

Jump to: Support · Sales · Customer success · Finance and operations · Product and onboarding · Engineering and internal reporting

Support

Support teams read a lot of text and write a lot of near-identical replies. Both are good first workflows, because an agent already reviews every reply before it goes out.

  • Ticket thread summarizerFor a support agent, when a ticket thread runs past ten messages, the workflow will read it and write the issue, what has been tried, and a suggested next step into the ticket sidebar, so that the agent can reply without rereading the history.
    Medium risk · Data: Zendesk, Intercom, or Help Scout · First Sprint: Yes
  • Reply drafter from your help centerFor a support agent, when a how-to question arrives, the workflow will find the matching help-center articles and draft a reply that links to them, so that common questions are answered in one pass.
    Medium risk · Data: Help center articles and past solved tickets · First Sprint: Yes
  • Ticket triageFor the support lead, when a ticket is created, the workflow will suggest a category, product area, and urgency, so that the queue is sorted before anyone opens it.
    Medium risk · Data: Helpdesk tickets and your tag history · First Sprint: Yes
  • Bug report writerFor the engineer on call, when support escalates a ticket, the workflow will turn the thread into a bug report with steps to reproduce, the account, and links to logs, so that engineering gets a usable issue the first time.
    Medium risk · Data: Helpdesk plus Linear, Jira, or GitHub Issues · First Sprint: Yes
  • Weekly support themesFor the product lead, at the end of each week, the workflow will group the week's tickets into themes with counts and example quotes, so that product decisions start from what customers actually report.
    Medium risk · Data: Helpdesk export · First Sprint: Yes

Sales

Sales workflows pay off fast because the inputs already exist as call transcripts and CRM records. Keep the rep as the one who sends anything to a prospect.

  • Call notes to CRMFor an account executive, when a sales call transcript is ready, the workflow will write the summary, next steps, and qualification fields into the CRM deal, so that the CRM stays current without after-call admin.
    Medium risk · Data: Gong, Fathom, or Zoom transcripts, and HubSpot or Salesforce · First Sprint: Yes
  • Account research briefFor an account executive, when a first call is booked, the workflow will compile the account's website, CRM history, and product signups into a one-page brief, so that the call starts informed.
    Medium risk · Data: CRM, signup data, and the prospect's public website · First Sprint: Yes
  • Follow-up email drafterFor an account executive, after a call, the workflow will draft the follow-up email from the transcript and the deal stage, so that follow-ups go out the same day.
    Medium risk · Data: Call transcripts and CRM deal stage · First Sprint: Yes
  • Inbound lead qualifierFor the sales team, when a demo request comes in, the workflow will score it against your ideal customer profile and write the reason for the score, so that reps call the best-fit accounts first.
    Medium risk · Data: Signup or demo form, enrichment data, and CRM · First Sprint: Yes
  • Security questionnaire drafterFor the founder, when a prospect sends a security questionnaire, the workflow will draft answers from past questionnaires and your policy documents and flag every answer it can't support, so that a two-day task becomes a review.
    High risk · Data: Past questionnaires and security policies · First Sprint: No. Compliance answers carry contract risk, so ship a lower-risk workflow first.

Customer success

Customer success runs on context scattered across the CRM, the helpdesk, and product analytics. Pulling it into one place before a call is the most common first win.

  • Pre-renewal account summaryFor the account owner, two weeks before a renewal, the workflow will pull the account's CRM notes, usage, and open tickets and write a one-page summary into the CRM record, so that the renewal call starts from the full picture.
    Medium risk · Data: CRM, product analytics, and helpdesk · First Sprint: Yes
  • At-risk account explainerFor a customer success manager, each week, the workflow will read the usage drops, tickets, and survey comments of accounts already flagged as at risk and write why each one looks at risk, so that outreach starts with a reason.
    Medium risk · Data: Product analytics, helpdesk, and CRM · First Sprint: Narrow slice first: explain accounts your existing rules already flag. Predicting churn is a separate project.
  • Quarterly review drafterFor a customer success manager, before a quarterly business review, the workflow will turn the account's usage numbers and the quarter's tickets into a draft review outline, so that prep starts from a draft.
    Medium risk · Data: Product analytics, helpdesk, and CRM · First Sprint: Yes
  • Feature request clusteringFor the product lead, each month, the workflow will group feature requests from CRM notes, tickets, and call transcripts into themes linked to the accounts that asked, so that roadmap calls can weigh requests by revenue.
    Medium risk · Data: CRM, helpdesk, and call transcripts · First Sprint: Narrow slice first: start with one source, usually the helpdesk.
  • Setup check-in drafterFor a customer success manager, when a new account stalls on a setup step, the workflow will draft a check-in email based on what the account has set up so far, so that the nudge is specific instead of generic.
    Medium risk · Data: Product events and CRM · First Sprint: Yes

Finance and operations

In a 5 to 50 person company, finance work usually lands on a founder. These workflows draft and explain; a person still checks every number.

  • Investor update assistantFor the founder, when the month's metrics and a few sentences of context are added, the workflow will draft the investor update covering metrics, risks, and asks, so that updates go out every month, on time.
    Medium risk · Data: Stripe, your finance sheet, or your accounting system · First Sprint: Yes
  • Runway scenario explainerFor the founder, when a change is proposed (a hire, a price change, a churn assumption), the workflow will run it against current burn and revenue and write what it does to runway in plain language, so that the decision is made with the number in front of everyone.
    Medium risk · Data: Your finance model or accounting system · First Sprint: Yes
  • Expense categorizerFor whoever does the books, when card and bank transactions sync, the workflow will suggest a category and a note for each and flag anything unusual, so that month-end close starts from a reviewed list.
    Medium risk · Data: Bank feed and Xero or QuickBooks · First Sprint: Yes
  • Software spend reviewFor the operations lead, each month, the workflow will read card transactions and flag duplicate tools, renewals coming up, and subscriptions nobody has logged into, so that unused spend gets cancelled before it renews.
    Medium risk · Data: Card or bank export and your accounting system · First Sprint: Yes
  • Contract terms extractorFor the founder, when a customer contract is signed, the workflow will pull the term, price, renewal date, notice period, and special terms into the CRM, so that renewals and obligations don't live only in PDFs.
    High risk · Data: Signed contracts and CRM · First Sprint: Not as stated. Renewal dates and prices only, checked by a person, is a medium-risk first Sprint.

Product and onboarding

These run inside your product, in front of your customers. They are the ones that show up in sales demos, and they need the clearest acceptance criteria.

  • Onboarding assistantFor a new user, when they finish signing up, the workflow will use their setup data to suggest the next step in your product's first key workflow, inside the app, so that more new accounts reach first value.
    Medium risk · Data: Your product database and event data · First Sprint: Yes
  • Plain-language filtersFor a user of your product, when they type a question such as "accounts over $10K with no login this month", the workflow will turn it into a filter on their data and show the result, so that they get answers without building a report.
    Medium risk · Data: Your product database or API · First Sprint: Narrow slice first: one object, read-only queries.
  • Report summaryFor a user of your product, when they open a dashboard, the workflow will write three sentences on what changed since last period and what it likely means, so that the numbers arrive with a reading.
    Medium risk · Data: Your product's reporting data · First Sprint: Yes
  • Import mapperFor a new customer, when they upload a CSV, the workflow will suggest how their columns map to your fields and flag the rows that won't import, so that onboarding doesn't stall on a spreadsheet.
    Medium risk · Data: The customer's file and your schema · First Sprint: Yes
  • First-draft generatorFor a user of your product, when they create a new record of your product's core type (a proposal, a job post, a campaign), the workflow will draft it from their past ones and a short prompt, so that they start from a draft instead of a blank page.
    Medium risk · Data: The user's past records in your product · First Sprint: Yes

Engineering and internal reporting

Internal workflows are the safest first Sprint: no customer sees the output, and the people reviewing it are the people who asked for it.

  • Weekly report generatorFor the founder or team lead, every Monday, the workflow will turn last week's product data and notes into the weekly internal report in your template, so that the report writes itself and someone only edits it.
    Low risk · Data: Product analytics and your notes · First Sprint: Yes
  • Release notes writerFor the product manager, when a release is tagged, the workflow will read the merged pull requests and tickets and draft customer-facing release notes and an internal changelog, so that every release gets announced.
    Low risk · Data: GitHub and Linear or Jira · First Sprint: Yes
  • Meeting notes to tasksFor a team lead, after an internal meeting, the workflow will turn the transcript into decisions and tasks with owners in your project tool, so that action items don't get lost.
    Low risk · Data: Meeting transcripts and Linear, Asana, or Jira · First Sprint: Yes
  • Internal docs answersFor a new hire, when they ask a question in Slack, the workflow will answer from your internal docs with links, and say when it doesn't know, so that onboarding questions stop interrupting senior people.
    Low risk · Data: Notion or Confluence, and Slack · First Sprint: Narrow slice first: one documentation space.
  • Error spike triageFor the engineer on call, when an error-rate alert fires, the workflow will group the errors, link them to recent deploys, and write a probable-cause summary, so that triage starts from a hypothesis.
    Low risk · Data: Sentry or your error tracker, and deploy history · First Sprint: Yes

How to pick the first one

Pick the workflow that does well on four questions:

  • It runs at least weekly, so the team feels the difference and you collect examples fast.
  • Its data sits in one system you can export today.
  • A person already reviews this kind of output, so a human check costs nothing new.
  • You can say what a good output looks like, in a sentence, before anything is built.

Then put a number on it. For an internal workflow: the hours a week the work takes today, times the loaded hourly cost, times 52. For a feature in your product: the deal or retention metric it moves. The Sprint page shows the worked example.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best AI use cases for a B2B SaaS company?

The narrow ones: one user, one input, one output, and a person who checks the result. Support thread summaries, call notes written into the CRM, pre-renewal account summaries, release notes, and in-app report summaries all fit that shape. Start with a workflow that runs at least weekly and whose data sits in one system you can export.

Should the first AI feature be internal or customer-facing?

Usually internal. An internal workflow is reviewed by the people who asked for it, so mistakes are cheap and the team learns what good output looks like. Customer-facing features come next, once you have a rubric and an evaluation set from the first one.

How long does it take to build one of these?

Ten business days for one narrow workflow, if codebase access and sample data are ready on Day 1. That's the FDE Sprint: scoped on Day 1, a working prototype on your data by Day 5, in your repo by Day 10. Broader versions take longer; the Sprint ships the first narrow slice.

Should we start with an AI agent instead?

Start with a workflow that has a human check, then add agent behavior where the workflow needs it: calling tools, looking things up in your systems, taking several steps. An agent that acts on customers without a person approving it is a poor first project, because nobody has measured yet what good output looks like.
